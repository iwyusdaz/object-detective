# Historical-thinking notes

## Source check (done 2026-10-27)

- **Digital Inquiry Group (DIG)**, https://www.inquirygroup.org/: the site says DIG is "building on two decades as the Stanford History Education Group" and that its *Reading Like a Historian* lessons "teach students to think critically about the past and build arguments from evidence". **Verified.**
- **History lessons page**, https://www.inquirygroup.org/history-lessons: lists classroom posters named **Sourcing**, **Contextualization**, **Corroboration** and **Close Reading**, plus a **Historical Thinking Chart** lesson. **Verified** (names only).
- The chart's detailed questions need a free login and were **not read**. The game therefore uses only the four skill names and its own wording. It does not reproduce DIG material.

## How the game maps the skills

| Skill (DIG name) | Where the learner does it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Close reading (here: close looking at an object) | Inspect: tools + observation/inference classification | Objects are read like documents. The marks on them are evidence |
| Sourcing | Archive: "Who made it / When / Why" for every document; sourcing questions on the 1971 memo and the Davenport record | Fictional documents are sourced like real ones, and labelled fictional |
| Corroboration | Evidence board: pinning findings and records to each claim as supports / contradicts / unclear | M4 (painted number vs serial number) and M1 (dates) need two sources |
| Contextualization | Light touch: the Davenport record's 1836 Patent Office fire; the 1971 memo written about 140 years after 1832 | Deliberately limited. The slice targets objectives 1–4 (DECISIONS.md D-05) |

## Design choices grounded in these notes
- "Can't tell yet" is a full verdict. It is used in both cases (T3, M5) and the transfer task.
- Confidence ratings make calibration visible (Brier score in the debrief). This is not part of DIG; it comes from the plan's assessment design.
- No automated grading of free text. The label is self-checked against a 4-criterion rubric and an exemplar.
