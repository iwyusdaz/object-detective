# Needs analysis

## Problem
Sourcing, corroboration and stating uncertainty are core historical-thinking and research skills. Most students meet artifacts only as flat photos with confident labels, and they treat the label as fact. Novices also blur what they *see* with what they *infer*, and they write overconfident claims.

## Who and where
- Grade 8–12 social studies, first-year undergraduate history / information literacy, online museum visitors (pre- or post-visit).
- A 30–40 minute class activity, or a self-paced museum web activity. Desktop first; phones supported (Low preset, stacked layout).

## What learners need to practise (and why a 3D game)
| Need | Why this format helps |
|---|---|
| Notice physical evidence (marks, numbers, wear, construction) | Real CC0 scans let learners turn, light and magnify the object. The catalog numbers on three objects are found by looking, not given |
| Separate observation from inference | Every finding comes with a classify step and why-feedback |
| Source and corroborate documents | A records drawer of real Smithsonian records plus labelled fictional documents of varied reliability |
| Calibrate certainty | "Can't tell yet" verdicts and confidence ratings, with a calibration score in the debrief |
| Write for the public with honest hedging | Label writer with sentence starters, a 4-criterion rubric and an exemplar, self-checked (no auto-grading) |

## Constraints
- No servers, no API keys, no data leaves the browser (local learning record only).
- Only CC0 Smithsonian items. Facts about real objects only from their records. Fiction is limited to the framing and is labelled.
- WCAG 2.1 AA for the HTML layer. Full text-mode parity.

## Out of scope
More cases (the content format supports adding them), multiplayer, user-uploaded objects, VR/AR.
