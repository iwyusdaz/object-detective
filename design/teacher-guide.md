# Teacher guide (40-minute plan)

**The Object Detective** · grades 8–12, first-year undergraduates · works in any current browser. Text mode works on low-end devices and with screen readers.

The Lantern Museum and its documents are **fictional**. The five objects are **real** Smithsonian objects (National Museum of American History), shown as CC0 3D scans and linked to their real records.

## Learning goals
Students will separate observation from inference, source documents, corroborate evidence across objects and records, and write a museum label that says how sure we are. The four skills follow the Digital Inquiry Group's (formerly the Stanford History Education Group) *Reading Like a Historian* approach: sourcing, contextualization, corroboration and close reading. This game is not a DIG product.

## Plan
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 min | Hook: show a confident museum label and ask, "How would you know if this is right?" |
| 3–8 min | Students play the **Welcome** pre-check (4 items, ungraded), then **Case 1** (tutorial) |
| 8–28 min | **Case 2** (individually or in pairs). Circulate and ask: "Is that an observation or an inference?" "Who wrote that document, and when?" |
| 28–33 min | **On your own** transfer task |
| 33–40 min | Discussion (below). Optionally students download their record from *My progress* |

## Discussion questions
1. Claim M4: the relay *has* a serial number. Why is the painted number still not it? What told you?
2. Claim M5: why is "can't tell yet" better than "refuted"? What source would settle it?
3. The 1971 memo is a museum document. Why isn't that enough to trust it?
4. Where were you "very sure but wrong"? What made you overconfident?
5. Read your label aloud. Which word shows how sure you are?

## Answer key (expert verdicts)
Case 1: T1 confirmed · T2 refuted (181411 is the catalog number) · T3 can't tell yet.
Case 2: M1 refuted (relay ca 1860, coil ca 1868) · M2 refuted (replacement model; original destroyed in the 1836 Patent Office fire) · M3 confirmed (motor and coil are patent models) · M4 refuted (335.588 = catalog number 335588; serial number is 589) · M5 can't tell yet (no maker in the record; family story only) · M6 confirmed (motor is about 38 cm).
Transfer: X1 confirmed · X2 refuted (ca 1870) · X3 refuted (from Western Union Corporation).

## Assessment notes
- Case 2 shows verdict accuracy, evidence linking and calibration. Labels are **not** auto-graded: use the 4-criterion rubric (accuracy, evidence cited, uncertainty language, clarity) for peer or teacher review.
- Data stays in each browser (`object-detective:*` keys). Nothing is sent to the teacher or anyone else. Students can export a JSON record.

## Accessibility
Text mode (header button) gives the same decisions with photos and descriptions. Settings: text size, high contrast, reduced motion, quality preset. Keys can be remapped (Menu → Controls and keys).
